# Section 4: Results
*Draft — cellSim paper*

---

All results presented in this section were obtained from simulation traces generated by the `run_all_scenarios` executable. Each scenario was initialised with N = 1,000 cells, all in BASELINE state (TP53 `+/+`, BRCA1 `+/-`, D1 = D2 = 1.0). Output files (Markdown + CSV) were produced automatically in `traces/`. Unless otherwise stated, results correspond to a single run with a fixed seed (deterministic mode).

---

## 4.1 Baseline Controls: Model Boundary Validation

### Scenario 01 — Stable Baseline (no mutations, no division)

With all mutation rates and division rates set to zero, the population remained exactly stable at N = 1,000 cells across all simulated years, with 0% neoplastic and TP53 `+/+` fraction at 100% throughout. D1 and D2 remained fixed at their initial values (1.0), confirming that **no instability accumulates in the absence of biological pressure**. This scenario validates that the model has no spurious state drift.

### Scenario 02 — Exponential Growth (no mutations, division = 15%)

Removing mutational pressure while activating division at 15% per cycle produced a 4.96× population increase over 10 simulated years (from 1,000 to 4,955 cells), with 0% neoplastic cells. This result confirms that the **cell division mechanism operates independently and correctly**, and that division alone does not induce neoplastic transformation.

### Scenario 03 — BRCA1 Collapse (μ_BRCA1 = 0.20)

With a high BRCA1 mutation rate, the entire population underwent extinction by year 50, confirming the model's implementation of **BRCA1–TP53 synthetic lethality**: loss of BRCA1 function is uniformly lethal in cells retaining TP53 activity. The apoptosis rate peaked in early years as BRCA1 `−/−` mutations accumulated rapidly under intact TP53 surveillance.

---

## 4.2 Neoplastic Phenotypes

### Scenario 04 — TP53 Invasion (μ_TP53 = 0.10)

At a high TP53 mutation rate, 98% of the surviving population was neoplastic by year 50, with 100% immortality among neoplastic cells. The surviving population was reduced to 462 cells. This demonstrates that **TP53 loss is the primary driver of neoplastic transformation** in the model, consistent with TP53's role as the most commonly mutated gene in human cancers (~50% of all malignancies).

### Scenario 05 — High Instability (δ_low = 0.10, δ_high = 0.50)

Increasing the instability deltas while keeping the same TP53 mutation rate as Scenario 04 produced a larger surviving population (573 cells vs 462) and a similarly high neoplastic fraction. The increase in population size relative to Scenario 04 is a direct consequence of the shared delta architecture: because D1 and D2 use the same delta parameter, any configuration that accelerates genomic instability (D1) also accelerates apoptotic evasion accumulation (D2). Faster D2 growth enables more cells to cross the apoptotic evasion threshold (D2 > θ_D2 = 5.0) before succumbing to extrinsic apoptosis, effectively converting what would be eliminated PRIMER cells into immortal TUMORAL cells.

## 4.3 Biologically Realistic Scenarios

### Scenario 09 — Balanced Reference Configuration

Scenario 09 (μ_BRCA1 = 0.05, μ_TP53 = 0.025, neoplasm_k = 0.05, division rate = 5%) serves as the **primary reference** for biologically plausible parameter calibration. Table 1 shows its temporal evolution.

|------|-------|-----------|----------|-------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| 0 | 1,000 | 0 | 1,000 | 0.0% | 100.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 1.0 |
| 10 | 894 | 0 | 894 | 0.0% | 80.9% | 18.2% | 0.9% | 3.0 |
| 17 | 784 | **1** | 783 | 0.1% | 77.6% | 21.9% | 0.5% | 5.5 |
| 20 | 750 | 2 | 748 | 0.3% | 75.3% | 23.5% | 1.2% | 8.2 |
| 30 | 619 | 67 | 552 | 10.8% | 61.9% | 26.8% | 11.3% | 17.2 |

| 43 | 611 | 257 | 354 | 42.1% | 40.6% | 17.0% | 42.4% | 28.9 |

*† Total cells (alive + accumulated dead). ‡ Neoplastic fraction computed over alive cells (2,018 / 2,120). All 2,018 neoplastic cells are 100% immortal at year 80.*

Several observations emerge from Scenario 09:

1. **Neoplastic onset (year 17)**: The first neoplastic cell appears at year 17, coinciding with D2 reaching a maximum of 5.5 — just above θ_D2 = 5.0. This demonstrates the threshold dynamics of the model.

2. **TP53 heterozygote plateau**: The TP53 `+/−` fraction stabilises between 20–27% for most of the simulation (years 10–40), reflecting ongoing conversion of `+/+` cells to `+/−` and simultaneous loss of `+/−` cells to the `−/−` state or to apoptosis.

3. **Neoplastic acceleration**: The neoplastic fraction grows from 0% at year 17 to 34% at year 40 — a 23-year window that corresponds to the clinically relevant period of cancer development from precancerous lesion to detectable malignancy.

4. **Immortality onset**: All neoplastic cells are 100% immortal from the moment of first transformation (year 17 onward), since the model requires D2 > θ_D2 as a precondition for neoplastic transformation. This is a key property of the model: **no non-immortal neoplastic cells are produced**.

## 4.4 Big Bang Mode: Explosive Neoplastic Transformation

Scenario 10 (μ_BRCA1 = 0.05, μ_TP53 = 0.02, neoplasm_k = 0.20, Big Bang enabled, 30 years) demonstrates the model's capacity to reproduce explosive tumour growth dynamics. Table 2 shows the temporal progression.

**Table 2. Temporal dynamics of Scenario 10 (Big Bang low, 30 years).**

| Year | Total | Alive | Neoplastic | Protected | % Neo (alive) | TP53 `−/−` | Max D2 | % Immortal |
|------|-------|-------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| 0 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 0 | 1,000 | 0.0% | 0.0% | 1.0 | — |
| 5 | 1,000 | 752 | **4** | 748 | 0.5% | 0.5% | 6.5 | **100%** |
| 10 | 1,000 | 597 | 39 | 558 | 6.5% | 6.5% | 21.0 | 100% |
| 15 | 1,000 | 544 | 154 | 390 | 28.3% | 28.3% | 36.0 | 100% |
| 18 | 1,035 | 579 | 264 | 315 | 45.6% | 45.6% | 45.0 | 100% |
| 20 | 1,112 | 656 | 383 | 273 | 58.4% | 58.4% | 51.0 | 100% |
| 24 | 1,438 | 982 | 767 | 215 | 78.1% | 78.1% | 63.0 | 100% |
| 27 | 1,935 | 1,479 | 1,299 | 180 | 87.8% | 87.8% | 72.0 | 100% |
| 30 | 2,881 | 2,425 | 2,277 | 148 | **93.9%** | 93.9% | 81.0 | **100%** |

Key findings from Scenario 10:

1. **Universal immortality from first transformation (year 5)**: The very first neoplastic cells (n = 4) are 100% immortal. As in the reference scenario, D2 > θ_D2 is a required precondition for transformation, so all neoplastic cells automatically possess apoptotic resistance.

2. **Rapid TP53 `−/−` dominance**: By year 20, 58.4% of the living population is TP53 `−/−`, compared to ~1% in the reference scenario at year 20. The higher TP53 mutation rate (μ_TP53 = 0.02 vs 0.025, similar) combined with the higher neoplasm_k (0.20 vs 0.05) drives transformation far more aggressively.

3. **Population explosion via Big Bang division**: From year 18 onward, total cell count grows super-linearly (1,035 → 1,112 → 1,438 → 1,935 → 2,881), driven by neoplastic cell division. The protected population simultaneously contracts (315 → 148), representing continuous conversion of healthy cells to the neoplastic phenotype.

4. **Clonal expansion rate**: Between years 20 and 30, the neoplastic population grew from 383 to 2,277 cells — a 5.9× increase in 10 years, representing a mean annual growth rate of approximately 19.5% per year in the tumour compartment.

5. **D2 linear growth**: Max D2 increases by approximately 3.0 units per year throughout the simulation (from 1.0 to 81.0 over 30 years), reflecting the constant δ_D2 increment in the saturated TP53 `−/−` regime.

The convergence of % Neoplastic and % TP53 `−/−` at 93.9% in Scenario 10 (vs 10.8% vs 11.3% in Scenario 09) confirms that in the model, **neoplastic transformation is essentially equivalent to terminal TP53 homozygous loss** under the tested parameter regime.

## 4.5 Model Reproducibility

Scenarios 10 and 14 were designed with identical parameters to validate reproducibility. Both produced identical population trajectories across all tracked metrics (population size, neoplastic fraction, TP53 state distribution, D1/D2 bounds), confirming that the Mersenne Twister seeding mechanism ensures **fully deterministic simulation output** for a given parameter set. This is a necessary property for scientific reproducibility and parameter sensitivity studies.
